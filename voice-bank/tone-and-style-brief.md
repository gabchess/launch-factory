# Tone & Style Brief: template

Derive this file from your own `corpus/` once it exists. Every observation
below should cite the corpus filename(s) it came from, the same way the
sections in this template are structured. Nothing in this file should be
guessed or imported from a competitor's voice: if the corpus does not support
a claim, leave it out and log the gap in `gaps.md`.

Writer seats check drafts against the numbered rules at the end of this file
and cite a rule number for each voice judgment. The reviewer defined in
`reviewer/` is the final arbiter: this brief flags drift, it does not decide.

## Sections a usable brief needs

1. **Sentence length and rhythm.** Do short declaratives carry the emphasis,
   or does the brand write in longer, subordinated sentences? Quote one or
   two examples per pattern, with their corpus citation.
2. **Vocabulary register.** Plain operator language vs. marketing language.
   Any coined or recurring labels the brand uses for its own concepts.
   Whether competitors are named directly, and in what tone.
3. **Opener patterns**, by channel. How does a homepage headline start
   compared to a blog post's first line, compared to an email's first
   sentence? These usually differ inside one brand; treat each channel
   separately rather than writing one universal "opener rule."
4. **Punctuation and emoji habits.** Where emoji appear (if at all), how
   density differs by channel, what gets bolded, which bullet glyphs recur.
5. **CTA style.** The exact shape of a call to action per channel: one link
   line, an imperative verb, whatever reassurance text travels with it (free
   trial, no credit card, cancel anytime).
6. **Humor level.** Deadpan, absent, warm, self-deprecating: name it, and
   note if it differs between official copy and an individual's personal
   voice (a founder's LinkedIn voice is often warmer than the company blog).
7. **How the brand references customers and pain points.** Second person or
   third person, concrete artifacts or abstractions, structural framing of
   pain vs. emotional framing.
8. **Register differences across channels.** A short comparison table: how
   the same brand sounds different in email vs. blog vs. social vs. an
   individual's spoken voice, each grounded in corpus citations.
9. **Rules for adapters.** The output of all the above: a short numbered
   list of checkable DO/DON'T rules, each with a corpus citation, that a
   writer seat can literally test a sentence against. This is the section
   every voice judgment in a run should cite by number.

## Worked example (fictional, illustrative only)

The block below shows the shape of a finished brief for a fictional B2B
product, Quorum Desk, built from a small fictional corpus. It is here to show
structure, not to be reused as a real brief for any actual product.

> **1. Sentence length and rhythm:** Short declaratives land the pivot after
> a longer setup sentence. "Not a guess. A decision record." (`03`).
>
> **2. Vocabulary register:** Plain, operator-facing language: "the deal,"
> "the notes," never "synergy" or "leverage." One coined label: "decision
> debt" (`02`).
>
> **3. Opener patterns:** Website headlines state the outcome first ("Every
> deal decision, in one place," `01`). Blog posts open on the reader's
> specific moment of friction ("You just left a deal review with three
> different opinions about what happens next," `02`).
>
> **4. Punctuation and emoji:** No emoji on the website or in email. Light,
> functional emoji on social only (`04`).
>
> **5. CTA style:** One imperative line ending in an arrow: "See a sample
> decision record ->" (`01`).
>
> **6. Humor level:** Dry, understated, one line maximum: "Still tracking
> deals in a spreadsheet? That's a decision, too." (`03`).
>
> **7. Customer references:** Second person, concrete artifacts ("the notes
> after the call," "the thread nobody read," `02`, `03`).
>
> **8. Register differences:** Website is shortest and most declarative;
> blog opens in the reader's scene before naming the product; social adds
> the only emoji in the corpus.
>
> **Rules for adapters:**
> 1. DO open blog content in the reader's specific moment of friction, not
>    with the product name. (`02`)
> 2. DON'T use hype adjectives ("revolutionary," "seamless"). State the
>    mechanism plainly instead. (`01`, `03`)
> 3. DO keep CTAs to one imperative line ending in an arrow. Never stack
>    competing CTAs. (`01`)
> 4. DON'T invent figures, pricing, or customer counts not present in the
>    corpus.

## When a real brand guide arrives

If the organization later produces an official brand or tone-of-voice guide,
it supersedes this inferred brief. Keep the corpus as historical evidence,
re-derive the brief against the real guide, and bump this file's version so
reviewers know which generation of rules a given draft was checked against.
