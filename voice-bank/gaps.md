# Gaps: Voice Bank

Fill this in alongside `provenance.md` once you build a real corpus. This file
is the honest list of what you could not collect, where the corpus is thin,
and what a real brand guide would add that inference from public copy cannot.
It exists so nobody mistakes an interim, inferred voice bank for an official
one.

## Sections to keep current

### Could not collect

List each wall you hit by name: a login-gated platform, a paywalled archive,
a channel you did not attempt. Say what you tried and what you got instead
(a search-indexed snippet is weaker evidence than the full post; label it
`[LOW CONFIDENCE]` if you keep it at all). Never guess at what is behind a
wall you could not open.

### Corpus balance issues

Call out registers backed by too little evidence: an email rule resting on
one sample, a founder's voice represented only by an old, dated post, a
channel with no customer-facing examples at all. A single data point is not
a pattern; say so instead of writing a confident rule on top of it.

### Missing vs. a real brand guide

List what a canonical brand guide would have that an inferred, public-corpus
brief cannot manufacture on its own:

- Official tone-of-voice dimensions ("we are X but not Y" sliders)
- An approved or banned vocabulary list
- Audience personas and per-segment register
- An approved-claims list (what numbers, figures, or comparisons are cleared
  for external use)
- Product naming and trademark rules
- Email program samples beyond whatever single seed you have

### What to ask for

A short, addressed list: who owns the real brand guide if one exists, what
additional samples would close the biggest gap above, and who can confirm
whether a stylistic quirk you noticed is deliberate or incidental.

## Why this file matters

`tone-and-style-brief.md` is a warning system derived from whatever corpus
you could actually assemble, not a substitute for the brand owner's own
judgment. This file is what keeps that distinction honest: it is the
difference between "the corpus suggests X" and "the brand guide says X." Keep
it current every time you add corpus items or learn that a rule rests on
thinner evidence than you thought.
